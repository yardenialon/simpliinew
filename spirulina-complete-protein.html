Protein

Is spirulina a complete protein?

Short answer: yes. Spirulina carries all nine essential amino acids and is one of the most protein-dense whole foods on earth — 60 to 70% protein by dry weight. Here's what that means, how much you actually get, and how to use it.

All nine essential amino acids — in one green ingredient.

A protein is "complete" when it contains every essential amino acid your body can't make on its own. Spirulina does — which puts it in rare company among plant foods, alongside soy and quinoa.

The definition

What makes a protein "complete."

Your body builds its own proteins from 20 amino acids, but nine of them are "essential" — you can only get them from food. A food is called a complete protein when it provides all nine in meaningful amounts. Most single plant foods are missing or low in at least one; spirulina isn't.

Quality & digestibility

Not just complete — easy to use.

Protein quality isn't only about amino acids; it's also about how well your body can get at them. Here spirulina has a quiet edge: unlike many plant cells, it has a soft cell wall rather than tough cellulose, so its protein and nutrients are highly accessible — no "cracking" required the way chlorella needs.

And because SimpliiGood is fresh-frozen, never heat-dried, its proteins and heat-sensitive nutrients stay intact. You're getting the amino acids in close to the state the living algae made them.

Putting it to work

How to get more protein from spirulina.

Spirulina shines as a complement that rounds out plant meals. A few easy ways to stack it:

  • Protein smoothie: blend 1–2 cubes with a scoop of plant protein, banana and nut butter.
  • Post-workout bowl: add cubes to a smoothie bowl with Greek-style yogurt or soy yogurt, oats and seeds.
  • Everyday greens: drop a cube into juice or a green smoothie to round out the amino-acid profile of your day.

A quick note. This is general nutrition information, not medical or dietary advice. Spirulina is a food, best used as part of a varied diet; it isn't intended to diagnose, treat, cure or prevent any disease.

Common questions

Spirulina protein, answered.

Is spirulina a complete protein?

Yes. It contains all nine essential amino acids, which is what defines a complete protein, and it's about 60–70% protein by dry weight. A serving is small, so treat it as a high-quality protein booster rather than a full protein shake.

How much protein is in spirulina?

A SimpliiGood 2-cube serving (28 g) has about 2 g of complete plant protein; a full tray (140 g) about 11 g. By dry weight spirulina is roughly 60–70% protein, among the highest of any whole food.

Does spirulina have all nine essential amino acids?

Yes — histidine, isoleucine, leucine, lysine, methionine, phenylalanine, threonine, tryptophan and valine are all present, which is why spirulina is classed as a complete protein.

Is spirulina a good protein source for vegans?

It's a useful complete plant protein that also brings iron, B vitamins and antioxidants. It works best alongside larger protein sources like legumes, tofu and whole grains rather than as your only source.

Is spirulina protein easy to digest?

Generally yes. Spirulina has a soft cell wall rather than tough cellulose, so its protein is easy to access, and fresh-frozen spirulina is never heat-dried, which helps keep its proteins intact.
