Spirulina vs. moringa.

Two popular green superfoods — but they're not the same thing, and they don't do the same job. Spirulina is a protein-packed algae; moringa is a vitamin-rich leaf. Here's the honest, side-by-side comparison.

The short version

Algae vs. leaf.

Spirulina is a blue-green algae grown in water — exceptionally high in complete protein and iron, with the blue antioxidant phycocyanin. Moringa comes from the dried leaves of the Moringa oleifera tree — a good source of vitamins A and C, calcium and fiber. Both are nutritious; they just fill different gaps.

Which to pick

So which is right for you?

Choose spirulina if you want…

  • Complete protein in a whole food
  • A dense plant source of iron
  • The blue antioxidant phycocyanin
  • Caffeine-free, and a flavor-free option

Choose moringa if you want…

  • More vitamin A and vitamin C
  • Extra calcium from a plant
  • More fiber from a whole leaf
  • A different green to rotate in

They're not mutually exclusive — plenty of people use both. But if your main goals are protein and iron, spirulina does more per serving, and fresh-frozen spirulina has a taste advantage: because it's never dried, it's virtually flavor-free, so it disappears into a smoothie where moringa's grassy note tends to come through.

Common questions

Spirulina vs. moringa, answered.

Is spirulina or moringa better?

Neither is universally better — they do different jobs. Spirulina is a complete, protein-dense algae rich in iron and phycocyanin; moringa is a leaf higher in vitamins A and C, calcium and fiber. Pick spirulina for protein and iron, moringa for leaf vitamins, or use both.

Which has more protein?

Spirulina — about 60–70% protein by dry weight and a complete protein, versus roughly 25% for moringa leaf powder.

Which has more iron?

Both are iron-rich, but spirulina is especially iron-dense for its weight (a 2-cube serving is ~10% DV, a tray ~50%). Pairing either with vitamin C helps you absorb the plant iron.

Can I take spirulina and moringa together?

Yes — they're complementary. Spirulina leans protein, iron and antioxidants; moringa leans leaf vitamins, calcium and fiber. Using both broadens your nutrient base.

Does moringa or spirulina taste better?

Both dried powders are earthy. Fresh-frozen spirulina is the exception — never dried, so it's virtually flavor-free and vanishes into a smoothie, while moringa's grassy, slightly bitter note is more noticeable.
