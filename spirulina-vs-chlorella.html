The comparison

Spirulina vs.
chlorella.

They're both green algae superfoods, and they're often confused — but they're genuinely different. Spirulina leads on complete protein and the antioxidant phycocyanin; chlorella leads on chlorophyll. Here's the honest side-by-side, and how fresh-frozen spirulina fits in.

In one line

Same color, different algae.

Spirulina is a blue-green algae (technically a cyanobacterium) prized for complete protein and its vivid blue antioxidant, phycocyanin. Chlorella is a green single-celled freshwater algae prized for very high chlorophyll — but it has a tough cell wall that must be broken ("cracked") for your body to absorb its nutrients. Spirulina has no such wall, so it's generally easier to digest.

Which to pick

Not either/or — it depends.

Choose spirulina if…

You want complete plant protein, iron and the antioxidant phycocyanin in an easy daily form. Fresh-frozen spirulina adds all of that to a smoothie without any fishy taste — a habit you'll actually keep.

Chlorella makes sense if…

You're specifically after its very high chlorophyll. Just note the taste is strong and the cell wall needs to be "broken" for absorption — look for "cracked cell wall" on the label.

Plenty of people use both — they're complementary. But if you want one green superfood that's protein-rich, antioxidant-rich and genuinely easy to use every day, fresh-frozen spirulina is hard to beat. See what spirulina actually does and why fresh beats powder.

Common questions

Spirulina vs. chlorella, answered.

What is the difference between spirulina and chlorella?

Spirulina is a blue-green algae known for complete protein and phycocyanin; chlorella is a green algae known for very high chlorophyll and a tough cell wall that must be cracked for absorption. Spirulina has no such wall, so it's generally easier to digest.

Which is better, spirulina or chlorella?

Neither is simply better — they're different. Spirulina for complete protein, iron and phycocyanin in an easy everyday form; chlorella mainly for its chlorophyll. Some people use both. SimpliiGood makes fresh-frozen spirulina, which is flavor-free and keeps its heat-sensitive compounds intact.

Can you take spirulina and chlorella together?

Yes — they're complementary whole foods and are often sold together, since each brings different nutrients. If you're pregnant, nursing, on medication or managing a condition, check with your doctor first.

Is spirulina or chlorella better for protein?

Spirulina is the standout for protein — roughly 60–70% by dry weight, with all nine essential amino acids. Chlorella is protein-rich too, but usually a bit lower. For a complete-protein boost in a smoothie, spirulina is the stronger pick.

Does spirulina or chlorella taste better?

Both taste strong and grassy as dried powders. The difference is the format: fresh-frozen spirulina is flash-frozen instead of heat-dried, so it's virtually flavor-free and disappears into a smoothie.
