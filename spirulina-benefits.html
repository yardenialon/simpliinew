Why spirulina

The benefits of
spirulina, honestly.

Spirulina is one of the most nutrient-dense foods on earth — complete plant protein, iron, and antioxidants, all for about 10 calories a serving. Here's what it actually offers, grounded in what's really inside it, and why fresh-frozen keeps more of it than powder.

The short version

A lot of nutrition, one ingredient.

Spirulina is a blue-green microalgae that humans have eaten for centuries. What makes it a genuine superfood isn't hype — it's density: a huge amount of protein, iron, antioxidants and micronutrients packed into very few calories. SimpliiGood is one ingredient — spirulina and water — grown in food-grade pools, Non-GMO Project Verified, vegan and gluten-free.

Common questions

Spirulina benefits, answered.

What are the benefits of spirulina?

Its benefits come from its density: complete plant protein with all nine essential amino acids, iron (10% DV per SimpliiGood serving), the blue antioxidant phycocyanin, chlorophyll, B vitamins and vitamin C — for about 10 calories a serving, with no fat or sugar.

Is spirulina good for you?

For most healthy adults, yes — it's a nutritious whole food (one ingredient, Non-GMO Verified, vegan, gluten-free) and a concentrated source of plant protein, iron and antioxidants. It complements a balanced diet rather than replacing it; check with your doctor if you're pregnant, on medication or managing a condition.

What is spirulina good for?

Mainly as an easy source of plant protein and iron, a daily antioxidant boost (phycocyanin, chlorophyll), and dense green nutrition for vegan and vegetarian diets — all in something that blends into a smoothie without the fishy taste of powder.

How much spirulina should you take a day?

A common everyday amount is a 2-cube serving (28 g) in one smoothie. Spirulina is a food, so there's no single required dose — more adds more nutrition, but there's no need to megadose. Follow your healthcare provider's guidance for a specific goal.

Does spirulina have side effects?

Quality spirulina is well tolerated by most healthy adults — choose a trusted, Non-GMO Verified source grown in controlled, food-grade conditions. People with PKU, those on blood thinners or immune medication, and anyone pregnant should talk to a doctor first. General information, not medical advice.

Is fresh-frozen spirulina better than powder for benefits?

Fresh-frozen keeps more of the heat-sensitive compounds (phycocyanin, chlorophyll) that drying degrades, and stays flavor-free so you'll use it daily. Powder still wins on shelf life and travel. See the full comparison.
