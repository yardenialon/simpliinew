Iron & nutrition

Spirulina & iron,
the honest version.

The short answer: spirulina is one of the more iron-rich plant foods by weight — a SimpliiGood 2-cube serving gives you 10% of the Daily Value for iron, and a full tray 50%. It's plant (non-heme) iron, so it's absorbed best alongside vitamin C — which the cubes already contain. Here's exactly how much iron spirulina has, how well your body uses it, and how it fits an iron-conscious diet.

Absorption

Getting the most from plant iron.

The iron in spirulina — like the iron in lentils, tofu and leafy greens — is non-heme iron. Your body absorbs non-heme iron less efficiently than the heme iron in meat. That's not a weakness of spirulina specifically; it's true of every plant source. The good news is that absorption is something you can influence at the table.

Vitamin C is the big lever. Eating vitamin C in the same meal can significantly increase how much non-heme iron your body takes up. This is where fresh-frozen spirulina has a quiet advantage: each serving already brings about 8 mg of vitamin C, and because the cubes are flavor-free you naturally blend them with fruit — orange, mango, berries, kiwi — stacking even more vitamin C into the same glass.

What to keep apart from an iron-rich meal: tea and coffee (tannins), and large calcium doses, can blunt non-heme iron absorption — so enjoy your spirulina smoothie a little apart from your afternoon coffee.

Helps absorption

  • Vitamin C (citrus, berries, kiwi, bell pepper)
  • Blending spirulina into a fruit smoothie
  • A varied, whole-food diet

Blunts absorption

  • Tea & coffee at the same meal (tannins)
  • Large calcium supplements with the meal
  • High-dose fiber supplements taken together
Spirulina & anemia

What to know, honestly.

Iron-deficiency anemia happens when the body doesn't have enough iron to make healthy red blood cells, which can leave you tired and run-down. It's a medical condition — it should be diagnosed with a blood test and managed by a healthcare professional, who may recommend dietary changes, supplements, or treatment of an underlying cause.

So where does spirulina fit? Spirulina is a food, not a treatment for anemia. What it can be is a genuinely useful plant source of iron within an iron-conscious diet — especially for people eating vegan or vegetarian, who rely entirely on non-heme iron. A daily 2-cube serving quietly contributes 10% of your iron DV along with complete protein and vitamin C, in something that tastes like a fruit smoothie rather than a supplement.

If you think you may be low in iron or anemic, the right first step is a test and a conversation with your doctor — then spirulina can be one easy, pleasant part of the everyday diet you build around their advice.

A quick, honest note. This article is general education, not medical advice. Iron-deficiency anemia should be diagnosed and managed by a qualified healthcare professional. SimpliiGood spirulina is a food and is not intended to diagnose, treat, cure or prevent any disease. Always talk to your doctor before making changes if you are managing a health condition, are pregnant, or take medication.

Common questions

Spirulina & iron, answered.

How much iron is in spirulina?

SimpliiGood fresh-frozen spirulina provides about 1.9 mg of iron per 2-cube serving (28 g) — 10% of the Daily Value — and about 9.5 mg per tray (50% DV). That makes it one of the more iron-dense plant foods by weight.

Is spirulina a good source of iron?

Yes. At 10% of the Daily Value per serving, spirulina compares well with many plant foods. Its iron is non-heme (plant) iron, which is absorbed less efficiently than heme iron from meat — so pairing it with vitamin C helps you get more from it.

Is the iron in spirulina well absorbed?

Spirulina contains non-heme iron, which the body absorbs less readily than animal (heme) iron. Absorption improves with vitamin C. SimpliiGood cubes already contain about 8 mg of vitamin C per serving, and blending them with fruit like orange, mango or berries adds more.

How much spirulina should I take for iron?

There's no special "iron dose" of spirulina. A 2-cube serving (28 g) contributes about 10% of the Daily Value for iron and fits easily into a daily smoothie. It's food, not a supplement to megadose — if you're managing an iron deficiency, follow the amount your healthcare provider recommends.

Is spirulina better than an iron supplement?

They're different tools. An iron supplement gives a concentrated, measured dose and is what clinicians use to correct a diagnosed deficiency. Spirulina is a whole food that contributes iron along with protein and vitamin C. It's not a replacement for a supplement or treatment your doctor has prescribed.

Does spirulina help with anemia?

Iron-deficiency anemia is a medical condition that should be diagnosed and managed by a healthcare professional. Spirulina isn't a treatment for anemia — but as a plant source of iron it can be part of an iron-conscious diet. If you suspect you're anemic, see your doctor for testing and a plan.
